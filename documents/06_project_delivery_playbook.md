# Project Delivery and Governance Playbook

**Classification:** internal  
**Department:** operations  
**Allowed roles:** employee, manager, ceo  
**Effective date:** 2026-01-15  
**Version:** 2.0

## Purpose

This playbook establishes a consistent lifecycle for planning, executing, monitoring, and closing business and technology projects.

## Standard workflow

1. Initiate the project with a business justification, sponsor, owner, scope, and expected outcome.
2. Define measurable success criteria, deliverables, dependencies, and target dates.
3. Break deliverables into tasks with assigned owners and acceptance criteria.
4. Identify risks, assumptions, constraints, and mitigation plans.
5. Review progress, blockers, and resource requirements on a scheduled basis.
6. Record decisions, scope changes, and stakeholder approvals in the project log.
7. Validate deliverables against acceptance criteria before release.
8. Close the project with documented outcomes, performance metrics, and lessons learned.

## Roles and responsibilities

- Project sponsor: provides business direction and approves major scope or funding decisions.
- Project owner: accountable for delivery, coordination, and reporting.
- Team member: completes assigned work and communicates risks or blockers.
- Manager: reviews team capacity, progress, and escalations.
- Executive leadership: oversees strategic alignment and approved portfolio-level reporting.

## Risk and issue management

Risks and issues must include a description, impact, owner, mitigation or next action, and target resolution date.

High-impact risks must be escalated through the designated management process.

## Change management

Changes to scope, schedule, cost, or acceptance criteria must be documented and reviewed by the authorized project owner or sponsor.

## Escalation

A blocker that threatens a committed milestone must be documented with its impact, owner, next action, and requested decision.

The AI assistant may draft an escalation ticket, but submitting or executing it must follow the configured approval and authorization policy.

## Project closure

Project closure requires confirmation of deliverables, outstanding issues, stakeholder acceptance, final results, and lessons learned.
